# Commodity Family Playbooks

## Purpose

This file gives the Commodity Agent practical mini-playbooks for different commodity families.

Each playbook defines:

- demand drivers;
- supply drivers;
- key evidence;
- typical bullish / bearish signals;
- traps;
- freshness needs;
- handoffs.

## General Playbook Template

For each commodity family, analyze:

1. Demand:
   - who consumes;
   - which countries matter;
   - which industries matter;
   - investment / government demand if relevant.

2. Supply:
   - who produces;
   - constraints;
   - spare capacity;
   - project pipeline;
   - policy constraints.

3. Inventories / reserves:
   - commercial stocks;
   - exchange stocks;
   - strategic reserves;
   - above-ground stocks;
   - geological reserves.

4. Curve / carry:
   - contango / backwardation;
   - roll yield;
   - storage;
   - futures product impact.

5. Macro:
   - USD;
   - rates;
   - inflation;
   - growth;
   - China;
   - liquidity.

6. Policy / geopolitics:
   - sanctions;
   - export controls;
   - tariffs;
   - producer policy;
   - strategic reserve actions.

7. Traps:
   - common false narratives;
   - bad instruments;
   - stale data.

8. Handoffs:
   - ETF;
   - Equity;
   - Macro;
   - Market Positioning;
   - Risk;
   - Investment Committee.

## 1. Oil / Refined Products

### Demand Drivers

Key demand drivers include:

- transport fuels;
- gasoline;
- diesel;
- jet fuel;
- petrochemicals;
- China demand;
- US demand;
- India demand;
- Europe demand;
- emerging market demand;
- strategic reserve refill;
- recession / demand destruction.

### Supply Drivers

Key supply drivers include:

- OPEC+ policy;
- Saudi / Gulf spare capacity;
- US shale response;
- offshore projects;
- sanctions;
- Russia / Middle East flows;
- refinery utilization;
- natural decline rates;
- capex cycle.

### Inventories and Flows

Watch:

- crude inventories;
- gasoline inventories;
- distillate inventories;
- Cushing;
- SPR;
- floating storage;
- export / import flows;
- refinery margins;
- crack spreads where relevant.

### Bullish Signals

- inventories drawing faster than seasonal norms;
- spare capacity low;
- demand resilient despite price;
- refined product tightness;
- backwardation supported by physical tightness;
- OPEC discipline with visible demand.

### Bearish Signals

- inventories building;
- demand destruction;
- shale / non-OPEC supply response;
- OPEC cohesion weakening;
- contango with weak physical demand;
- refinery demand weakening.

### Common Traps

- assuming OPEC fully controls price;
- treating a supply shock as structural;
- ignoring refined product demand;
- ignoring SPR effects;
- ignoring rerouted sanctioned flows;
- using spot oil thesis for a futures ETF without roll analysis.

### Preferred Sources

- EIA;
- IEA;
- OPEC;
- exchange futures data;
- official inventory data;
- customs / trade data;
- tanker-flow providers where accessible;
- reputable energy market data providers.

### Handoffs

- Macro for growth / inflation / USD;
- ETF for oil ETFs;
- Equity for energy producers;
- Market Positioning for futures crowding;
- Risk for geopolitical or leveraged exposure.

## 2. Natural Gas / LNG

### Demand Drivers

Key demand drivers include:

- power generation;
- heating;
- industrial demand;
- LNG import demand;
- Europe storage;
- Asia demand;
- weather;
- fuel switching.

### Supply Drivers

Key supply drivers include:

- gas production;
- pipeline capacity;
- LNG export capacity;
- liquefaction / regasification;
- outages;
- associated gas from oil production;
- storage constraints.

### Bullish Signals

- low storage before peak season;
- cold winter or hot summer demand;
- LNG demand competing across regions;
- production outages;
- pipeline bottlenecks;
- strong power burn.

### Bearish Signals

- storage surplus;
- mild weather;
- production growth;
- LNG capacity delays reducing feedgas demand;
- weak industrial demand.

### Common Traps

- treating regional gas markets as one global market;
- ignoring transport constraints;
- extrapolating weather;
- confusing Henry Hub, European TTF, and Asian LNG exposure;
- ignoring storage limits.

### Preferred Sources

- EIA;
- IEA;
- official storage data;
- regional grid / energy agencies;
- LNG market data providers where accessible;
- exchange futures data.

### Handoffs

- Macro for recession / industrial demand;
- ETF for gas ETFs;
- Risk for volatile futures products;
- Market Positioning for crowded seasonal trades.

## 3. Gold / Precious Metals

### Demand Drivers

Gold is a monetary commodity.

Demand drivers include:

- central bank buying;
- reserve diversification;
- ETF flows;
- jewelry demand;
- investment demand;
- safe-haven demand;
- real rates;
- USD;
- inflation / currency debasement concerns.

Silver / platinum / palladium may also include industrial demand.

### Supply Drivers

Supply drivers include:

- mine supply;
- recycling;
- above-ground stocks;
- producer hedging;
- project pipeline.

### Bullish Signals

- falling real rates;
- weaker USD;
- persistent central bank buying;
- ETF inflows;
- geopolitical risk;
- currency confidence concerns;
- supply growth limited.

### Bearish Signals

- rising real rates;
- stronger USD;
- ETF outflows;
- jewelry demand weakening;
- crowded positioning;
- central bank buying slowing.

### Common Traps

- “central banks buy, therefore gold must rise”;
- confusing gold bullion with miners;
- ignoring real rates;
- ignoring ETF flows;
- treating gold as a perfect inflation hedge;
- ignoring price sensitivity of jewelry demand.

### Preferred Sources

- World Gold Council;
- central bank reserve data;
- IMF-style reserve datasets where available;
- exchange data;
- ETF issuer data;
- CFTC positioning;
- official rate / inflation data.

### Handoffs

- Macro for real rates / USD / inflation;
- ETF for gold ETFs;
- Equity for miners and royalty companies;
- Market Positioning for crowding;
- Portfolio Fit for hedge role.

### Demand Drivers

Demand drivers include:

- construction;
- China property;
- grid investment;
- EVs;
- renewables;
- AI / data centers;
- electronics;
- manufacturing;
- defense;

### Supply Drivers

Supply drivers include:

- ore grades;
- disruptions;
- smelting / refining;
- treatment charges where relevant;

- project pipeline;

- refining bottlenecks;

### Bearish Signals

- ignoring China cycle;
- ignoring inventory rebuild;
- confusing mine supply with refined supply;
- ignoring substitution;
- ignoring long project lead times;
- ignoring battery chemistry changes.

### Preferred Sources

- LME / CME / SHFE data;
- USGS;
- company filings;
- mine project data;
- industry data providers;
- customs / trade data;
- official industrial production data.

### Handoffs

- Equity for miners;
- ETF for metals ETFs;
- Sector for industrial themes;

## 5. Uranium

### Demand Drivers

Demand drivers include:

- reactor demand;
- nuclear restarts;
- new reactor builds;
- utility contracting;
- policy support;
- energy security;
- strategic inventories.

### Supply Drivers

Supply drivers include:

- mine production;
- restarts;
- project development;
- secondary supply;
- inventories;
- conversion;
- enrichment;
- sanctions;
- producer discipline.

### Market Structure

Uranium requires special attention to:

- term market vs spot market;
- utility contracting cycle;
- physical trusts / funds;
- enrichment and conversion bottlenecks;
- long project timelines.

### Bullish Signals

- utilities contracting aggressively;
- mine restarts delayed;
- inventories declining;
- policy support for nuclear;
- enrichment / conversion bottlenecks;
- physical trust buying.

### Bearish Signals

- contracting slows;
- supply restarts faster than expected;
- policy delays;
- secondary supply emerges;
- physical trust demand weakens.

### Common Traps

- treating uranium like ordinary industrial metal;
- over-relying on spot price;
- ignoring term contracts;
- ignoring fuel-cycle bottlenecks;
- ignoring opaque inventories;
- treating nuclear policy announcements as immediate demand.

### Preferred Sources

- nuclear industry data;
- IAEA / WNA-style sources where available;
- company filings;
- mine project data;
- utility contracting commentary;
- ETF / physical trust disclosures.

### Handoffs

- ETF for uranium ETFs / trusts;
- Equity for uranium miners;
- Risk for liquidity and opaque data;
- Macro / Policy where energy security is central.

## 6. Agriculture / Softs

### Demand Drivers

Demand drivers include:

- food demand;
- feed demand;
- biofuel demand;
- export demand;
- country import needs;
- substitution among crops;
- currency and freight.

### Supply Drivers

Supply drivers include:

- acreage;
- yield;
- weather;
- crop calendar;
- harvest progress;
- planting conditions;
- pests / disease;
- fertilizer and input costs;
- export restrictions;
- storage.

### Core Metrics

Use:

- stocks-to-use;
- acreage;
- yield;
- production;
- ending stocks;
- export sales;
- crop condition;
- weather and regional harvest data.

### Bullish Signals

- stocks-to-use tightening;
- weather damage;
- export restrictions;
- strong import demand;
- crop condition deterioration;
- currency support for exports.

### Bearish Signals

- bumper harvest;
- weak export demand;
- stocks-to-use rising;
- favorable weather;
- substitution away from expensive crop;
- policy easing.

### Common Traps

- extrapolating weather shocks;
- ignoring crop calendar;
- confusing local shortage with global shortage;
- ignoring substitution;
- ignoring currency and freight;
- treating one WASDE change as permanent.

### Preferred Sources

- USDA WASDE;
- USDA crop progress;
- FAO;
- official agriculture ministries;
- customs / export data;
- weather agencies;
- exchange data.

### Handoffs

- Macro for food inflation;
- Risk for weather / policy uncertainty;
- ETF for agriculture funds;
- Market Positioning for crowded futures positioning.

## 7. Broad Commodity Baskets

### Demand / Exposure Drivers

Broad baskets are not automatically diversified.

Analyze:

- energy weight;
- metals weight;
- agriculture weight;
- precious metals weight;
- index methodology;
- futures contract selection;
- roll methodology;
- rebalance schedule;
- collateral yield where relevant.

### Bullish Signals

- broad-based commodity tightness;
- inflation regime support;
- multiple sectors showing inventory tightness;
- favorable curve environment.

### Bearish Signals

- exposure dominated by weak bucket;
- adverse roll yield;
- methodology mismatch;
- inflation hedge not working;
- high energy concentration when user wants diversified commodity exposure.

### Common Traps

- calling a basket diversified without checking weights;
- ignoring roll methodology;
- ignoring energy concentration;
- ignoring ETF wrapper costs;
- assuming inflation hedge always works;
- confusing commodity basket with commodity producers.

### Preferred Sources

- index methodology documents;
- ETF issuer holdings;
- exchange futures data;
- ETF disclosures;
- official commodity data by bucket.

### Handoffs

- ETF Agent for fund wrapper;
- Portfolio Fit for diversification role;
- Macro for inflation regime;
- Risk for structure and drawdown risk.
