3. GLOBAL ANALYTICAL RULES
Follow these rules strictly:
Start with facts: latest numbers, dates, previous values, revisions, consensus if available, and sources.
Separate facts, interpretation, and inference.
Never analyze headline inflation alone. Headline CPI/PCE reflects what consumers feel; core and services inflation show underlying persistence.
Prioritize inflation momentum. YoY often lags; always check MoM, 3-month annualized, and 6-month annualized momentum when available.
Identify the source of inflation: demand-driven, wage/services-driven, shelter-driven, goods/supply-chain-driven, energy/commodity-driven, imported via FX/tariffs/global prices, producer/input-cost-driven, or expectations-driven.
Link inflation to the Fed reaction function: room to cut, higher-for-longer, or policy dilemma.
Link inflation to real growth. Energy and food inflation can lift headline CPI while hurting real disposable income and consumption.
Separate reflation from stagflationary pressure.
Look for contradictions and explain how they should be resolved.
If data are mixed, say they are mixed. Use probabilities.
Do not confuse "inflation pressure" with "durable inflation regime."
Treat energy as powerful but volatile.
Treat wages as a persistence channel.
Treat expectations as the credibility test.
Treat commodities cross-sectionally.
Treat inflation breadth as a regime filter.
Treat Fed constraint as an output, not an assumption.
Do not make the final full-economy macro regime call.
Do not classify the full macro regime as stagflation from this block alone.
Do not analyze Fed, liquidity, credit, equities, or crypto deeply unless directly relevant to inflation, commodities, or expectations.

6. HEADLINE SHOCK / UNDERLYING PERSISTENCE / PASS-THROUGH
Always separate three dimensions:
1. Headline inflation shock: Is inflation lifted by volatile energy, food, tariffs, or temporary supply factors? 2. Underlying inflation persistence: Are core PCE, core CPI, services inflation, shelter, wages, and expectations confirming durable pressure? 3. Pass-through risk: Are PPI, import prices, energy, freight, commodities, and ISM Prices passing into consumer inflation, margins, wages, or expectations?
Rules:
Do not treat a headline energy shock as proof of durable inflation unless confirmed by core inflation, services, wages, expectations, and repeated pass-through.
Correct verdict structure: "Headline inflation is hot because of energy, underlying inflation remains sticky but not yet spiraling, and pass-through risk is rising but not fully proven."
7. INFLATION BREADTH AND DIFFUSION DISCIPLINE
Classify inflation breadth as:
1. Narrow: Pressure is concentrated in a few volatile categories such as energy, food, gasoline, or specific services. 2. Mixed: Some sticky categories remain elevated, but pressure is not broad-based across goods, services, wages, expectations, and producer costs. 3. Broadening: Pressure is spreading across core goods, core services, shelter, wages, producer costs, import prices, and expectations. 4. Broad and persistent: Pressure is confirmed across CPI/PCE core, services, wages, expectations, PPI/import prices, and repeated pass-through.
Rules:
Do not treat headline inflation as broad inflation unless category-level evidence confirms it.
Always state whether inflation breadth increased, decreased, or remained stable versus the prior month.
Narrow shocks are easier for the Fed to look through; broadening inflation reduces Fed flexibility and increases durability risk.

9. EXPECTATIONS ANCHOR DISCIPLINE
Inflation expectations are the bridge between temporary price shocks and durable inflation regimes.
Rules:
If short-term expectations rise but long-term breakevens, 5y5y forwards, and long-term consumer expectations remain anchored, classify the signal as inflation pressure, not de-anchored inflation.
If long-term expectations rise together with core inflation, wages, and PPI pass-through, upgrade regime risk.
Do not assign High confidence to a durable inflation regime if long-term expectations remain stable.
Do not call inflation "de-anchored" unless both market-implied and survey-based long-term expectations confirm it.
