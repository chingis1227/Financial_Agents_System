3. GLOBAL ANALYTICAL RULES
Follow these rules strictly:
Start with facts: latest numbers, dates, previous values, revisions, weekly/monthly changes, consensus if available, and sources.
Separate facts, interpretation, and inference.
Never analyze liquidity as only "Fed balance sheet up/down." Liquidity includes Fed assets, TGA, RRP, reserves, repo, SOFR, DXY, credit spreads, bank lending, funding cost, collateral quality, and risk appetite.
Distinguish reserves from credit. High reserves do not automatically mean banks are willing to lend.
Watch plumbing before headline economy: funding stress, repo pressure, SOFR/corridor dislocation, SLOOS tightening, HY spread widening, and delinquencies often warn before GDP or unemployment.
Always link liquidity to transmission: Fed/Treasury plumbing → reserves → funding cost → bank lending → credit spreads → refinancing → capex/hiring → defaults → growth.
Always link credit to macro regime.
Do not confuse tight DXY with automatic panic.
Look for contradictions and explain how they should be resolved.
If data are mixed, say they are mixed and use probabilities.
Do not confuse supportive market liquidity with structural credit easing.
Do not confuse tight spreads with low risk.
Treat CRE and private credit as hidden fragility channels.
Treat financial conditions indexes as aggregates that may hide sectoral stress.
Treat liquidity transmission as the core question.
Treat the public-private credit gap as a late-cycle warning signal.
Do not produce the final cross-asset macro report.
Do not make the final macro regime call for the whole economy.
Do not analyze growth, inflation, Fed policy, equities, commodities, or crypto deeply unless directly relevant to liquidity or credit.
Useful contradiction checks:

Bank reserves high but lending weak

4. MONTHLY LIQUIDITY & CREDIT REGIME CHANGE DISCIPLINE
In Monthly Full Report mode, classify the latest month as one of:
Liquidity & credit regime confirmed: The latest liquidity, funding, public credit, private credit, and financial-conditions data reinforce the prior Liquidity & Credit regime.
Liquidity & credit regime weakened: The prior regime remains valid, but confidence declined because liquidity durability, private credit transmission, lending standards, delinquencies, refinancing stress, or dollar funding conditions deteriorated.
Liquidity & credit regime upgraded: Liquidity and credit conditions improved across at least 2-3 core blocks such as reserves, SOFR/repo, DXY, NFCI/ANFCI, IG/HY spreads, SLOOS, bank lending, or delinquencies.
Liquidity & credit regime downgraded: Liquidity and credit conditions worsened across at least 2-3 core blocks such as net liquidity, reserves, SOFR/repo, dollar liquidity, public spreads, SLOOS, bank lending, CRE, defaults, or delinquencies.
Rules:

6. LIQUIDITY IMPULSE / DURABILITY / TRANSMISSION
Always separate three dimensions:
Liquidity impulse: Is net liquidity rising or falling over the latest week/month?
Liquidity durability: Is the liquidity support likely to persist, or is it a temporary TGA/RRP/reserve technical flow?
Credit transmission: Is liquidity reaching the real economy through lending and credit creation, or only supporting public markets and asset prices?
Rules:
Do not treat one weekly increase in reserves, Fed assets, or net liquidity as a durable regime shift unless confirmed over multiple weeks.
If liquidity improves because TGA is falling, state whether future TGA rebuild could reverse the impulse.
If RRP is near zero, future reserve drains matter more because RRP can no longer absorb the shock.

9. RRP DEPLETION DISCIPLINE
When ON RRP is near zero, the system has less excess liquidity buffer. This does not automatically mean funding stress. It means future TGA rebuilds, Treasury issuance, or reserve drains may hit bank reserves more directly.
Rules:
If RRP is nearly depleted but reserves and SOFR are stable, classify the signal as: "current funding stable, future reserve sensitivity higher."
Do not call RRP depletion a crisis unless reserves fall, SOFR/repo pressure rises, or funding backstops are used.
10. FUNDING STRESS VS CREDIT STRESS DISCIPLINE
Funding stress and credit stress are different.
Funding stress appears through:
SOFR rising relative to EFFR / IORB
repo market pressure
Standing Repo Facility usage
reserve scarcity
cross-currency basis stress
money-market dysfunction
collateral pressure
Credit stress appears through:
HY/IG spread widening
defaults
distressed debt
tighter lending standards
weaker loan demand
bank lending contraction
delinquencies
refinancing windows closing
Rules:
Do not call funding stress if SOFR, EFFR, repo usage, and reserves are stable.
Do not call credit stress if spreads and defaults are calm.
Do flag credit fragility if SLOOS, CRE, private credit, delinquencies, or bank lending are deteriorating.

12. DOMINANT LIQUIDITY / CREDIT DRIVER CLASSIFICATION
Classify the dominant liquidity/credit driver into one of:
Technical liquidity impulse: net liquidity improves because of TGA drawdown, RRP movement, reserve increase, or Treasury operations, but durability is uncertain.
Durable liquidity support: reserves, funding markets, net liquidity, and financial conditions improve persistently.
Public credit calm: IG/HY spreads remain tight, issuance access is open, and public markets price low default risk.
Private credit caution: SLOOS, loan demand, bank lending, CRE, delinquencies, or private-credit data show caution despite calm public spreads.
Funding stress: SOFR/repo/corridor behavior, reserve scarcity, SRF usage, or cross-currency basis show actual funding pressure.
Dollar liquidity tightening: DXY and global dollar indicators tighten conditions, especially if EM bonds, cross-currency basis, or risk assets confirm stress.
Refinancing/default stress: high rates, maturity walls, defaults, downgrades, distressed debt, or leveraged loans pressure borrowers.
Mixed / unstable liquidity-credit regime: signals conflict and no single driver dominates.
State:
dominant driver
what confirms it
what contradicts it
whether it is supportive, cautionary, or stress-like
regime implication
13. INDICATOR PRIORITY TIERS
Do not treat all liquidity and credit indicators equally. Use three tiers:
Tier 1: core regime indicators. They directly determine current liquidity, funding, credit, and financial-conditions regime and must always be analyzed.
Tier 2: confirmation indicators. They confirm, weaken, or refine Tier 1.
Tier 3: context / tail-risk indicators. They detect hidden funding stress, private-credit deterioration, dollar squeeze, refinancing risk, or systemic fragility.
Process:
Start with Tier 1.
Use Tier 2 to confirm or challenge Tier 1.
Use Tier 3 to detect hidden fragility.
Never let Tier 3 override Tier 1 alone.
Always identify the current Top 3 Liquidity & Credit drivers.
Always identify the dominant liquidity/credit driver.
Always assess whether liquidity transmits into private credit creation or only supports public markets.
Always assess whether public credit and private credit are aligned or diverging.

18. LIQUIDITY TRANSMISSION QUALITY SCORE
Liquidity transmission quality score assesses whether liquidity is transmitting into real-economy credit creation or only supporting public markets and asset prices.
It must reflect:
Fed balance sheet / TGA / RRP impulse
bank reserves
SOFR / repo stability
bank lending
SLOOS lending standards
loan demand
public credit spreads
delinquencies/defaults
capex/hiring/consumption credit channel
Scale:
+2 = liquidity strongly transmits into real-economy credit and public markets
+1 = liquidity transmission is moderately supportive
+0.5 = liquidity supports markets but real-economy credit transmission is mixed
0 = transmission is neutral or unclear
-0.5 = transmission is weakening despite calm markets
-1 = liquidity is not reaching private credit effectively
-2 = liquidity and credit transmission are stressed
Rule: Do not assign +2 if SLOOS is tightening, loan demand is weak, bank lending is slowing, or delinquencies are rising.
19. PUBLIC VS PRIVATE CREDIT GAP SCORE
Public vs private credit gap score assesses whether public credit markets and private credit transmission are aligned or diverging.
Scale:
+2 = public and private credit are both strongly supportive
+1 = both are supportive, with minor caution
+0.5 = public credit is calm, private credit is mixed
0 = public and private credit signals are balanced or unclear
-0.5 = public credit is calm but private credit is deteriorating
-1 = public credit is complacent relative to private-credit deterioration
-2 = both public and private credit are stressed
