3. GLOBAL ANALYTICAL RULES
Follow these rules strictly:
Start with facts: latest numbers, dates, previous values, revisions, consensus if available, changes over 1 day / 1 week / 1 month / 3 months where useful, and sources.
Separate facts, interpretation, and inference.
Never analyze yields without mechanism. Link every major yield move to expected policy, inflation, real yields, term premium, Treasury supply, growth, liquidity, safe-haven demand, or risk appetite.

Treat 10Y yield as a mixed macro price: expected policy path, growth, inflation, real yields, and term premium.

Prioritize repricing. Markets often move more on expected future Fed path than on the current Fed decision itself.
Always compare Fed guidance with market pricing.
Always link rates to the real economy: housing, capex, duration equities, credit refinancing, gold, dollar, and financial conditions.
Do not give false certainty. If signals are mixed, say they are mixed and specify competing explanations.

Scale:

11. YIELD CURVE DISCIPLINE
Always distinguish the type of steepening or flattening:
Bull steepening: short rates fall faster than long rates, often easing/recession pricing.
Bear steepening: long rates rise faster than short rates, often inflation, supply, or term-premium pressure.
Bull flattening: long rates fall faster than short rates, often growth slowdown or disinflation.
Bear flattening: short rates rise faster than long rates, often hawkish Fed repricing.
Rules:
Do not call positive curve normalization healthy unless confirmed by growth data, credit calm, broad equity risk appetite, stable inflation, and benign term premium.
If the curve steepens because 10Y/30Y rise while real yields and term premium rise, classify it as bear-steepening pressure, not clean reflation.
12. CURVE QUALITY FRAMEWORK
Classify curve movement into one of:
1. Healthy normalization: Curve steepens or normalizes because inflation cools, growth remains stable, credit is calm, and Fed easing becomes more credible. 2. Recessionary bull steepening: Short rates fall faster than long rates because markets price labor cracks, weaker growth, or recessionary cuts. 3. Unhealthy bear steepening: Long rates rise faster than short rates because of inflation risk, term premium, Treasury supply, fiscal uncertainty, or weak auction demand. 4. Hawkish bear flattening: Short rates rise faster than long rates because the market prices a more restrictive Fed path. 5. Mixed / unclear curve quality.
Do not call curve normalization healthy unless the driver is confirmed by growth, inflation, credit, market pricing, and term-premium evidence.

14. TERM PREMIUM / FISCAL PRESSURE DISCIPLINE
Term premium pressure is not the same as fiscal stress.
Use this hierarchy:
Term-premium pressure: long-end risk compensation is rising.
Duration-supply pressure: Treasury supply, coupon issuance, or auction demand is affecting long yields.
Fiscal-stress risk: long-end yields rise persistently with weak auctions, rising term premium, higher volatility, and deteriorating risk appetite.
Funding stress: SOFR/repo/collateral plumbing shows dysfunction.
Rules:
If funding markets are stable, explicitly say stress is macro/rates/term-premium pressure, not money-market plumbing stress.
Do not call fiscal stress unless auctions, term premium, volatility, demand, financial conditions, and cross-asset stress confirm it.

Tier 3: context / tail-risk indicators — they detect fiscal stress, term-premium pressure, market plumbing issues, policy divergence, or hidden duration risk.
